# The French Revolution

## Overview

The French Revolution (1789-1799) was a period of radical social and political upheaval in France that had a lasting impact on French history and the world. It marked the end of absolute monarchy, the rise of democracy and nationalism, and the birth of modern political ideologies.

## Causes

### Social Structure (Old Regime)
French society was divided into three estates:
- **First Estate**: Clergy (1% of population, owned 10% of land, tax-exempt)
- **Second Estate**: Nobility (2% of population, owned 25% of land, tax-exempt)
- **Third Estate**: Everyone else (97% of population, paid all taxes)

### Economic Crisis
- France was nearly bankrupt from wars (especially supporting American Revolution)
- Poor harvests led to food shortages and rising bread prices
- Heavy taxation burden on the Third Estate
- King Louis XVI's lavish spending at Versailles

### Enlightenment Ideas
Philosophers like Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu promoted:
- Individual liberty
- Separation of powers
- Popular sovereignty
- Reason over tradition

### Political Inequality
- Absolute monarchy with no representative government
- Third Estate had no real political power despite being the majority
- Growing educated middle class (bourgeoisie) demanded political representation

## Key Events

### 1789: The Revolution Begins

**May**: Estates-General convened for first time since 1614
**June 17**: Third Estate declares itself the National Assembly
**June 20**: Tennis Court Oath - vow not to disband until constitution is written
**July 14**: Storming of the Bastille (now celebrated as Bastille Day)
**August 4**: Abolition of feudalism
**August 26**: Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen adopted

### 1790-1792: Constitutional Monarchy

- **1790**: Civil Constitution of the Clergy subordinates Church to state
- **June 1791**: Royal family attempts to flee (Flight to Varennes) but is captured
- **September 1791**: New constitution establishes constitutional monarchy
- **April 1792**: France declares war on Austria
- **August 1792**: Mob storms Tuileries Palace; monarchy suspended

### 1792-1794: The Republic and Terror

- **September 1792**: National Convention proclaims First French Republic
- **January 1793**: King Louis XVI executed by guillotine
- **October 1793**: Queen Marie Antoinette executed
- **1793-1794**: Reign of Terror under Robespierre
  - Committee of Public Safety takes control
  - 16,000-40,000 executed during the Terror
  - Revolutionary calendar introduced
  - Dechristianization campaign
- **July 1794**: Robespierre executed; Terror ends

### 1795-1799: The Directory

- New constitution creates five-person Directory
- Economic instability and corruption
- Military victories abroad but chaos at home
- Rise of Napoleon Bonaparte as military hero

### 1799: Napoleon's Coup

- **November 1799**: Napoleon Bonaparte seizes power in coup d'état
- End of the Revolution; beginning of Napoleonic era

## Major Figures

### Revolutionaries
- **Maximilien Robespierre**: Leader during the Terror
- **Georges Danton**: Moderate revolutionary leader
- **Jean-Paul Marat**: Radical journalist and politician
- **Marquis de Lafayette**: Commander of National Guard

### Royalty
- **Louis XVI**: King of France, executed 1793
- **Marie Antoinette**: Queen, executed 1793

### Military
- **Napoleon Bonaparte**: General who eventually became Emperor

## Key Documents

### Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen (1789)
Fundamental document asserting:
- Natural rights (liberty, property, security, resistance to oppression)
- Equality before the law
- Popular sovereignty
- Freedom of speech and press
- Due process

## Impact and Legacy

### In France
- End of absolute monarchy and feudalism
- Establishment of principle of popular sovereignty
- Secularization of society
- Legal equality (at least in principle)
- Metric system introduced
- Napoleonic Code (legal framework still in use)

### Worldwide
- Inspired democratic movements globally
- Concept of citizen rights spread
- Nationalism as a political force
- Model for future revolutions (1848 revolutions, Latin American independence)
- Demonstrated power of mass political mobilization

### Long-term Effects
- Growth of political ideologies (liberalism, conservatism, socialism)
- Modern concepts of left and right wing politics
- Secularism in government
- Human rights as universal principles
- Nation-state as primary political unit

## Revolutionary Symbols

- **Tricolor Flag**: Blue, white, and red - still France's flag
- **"La Marseillaise"**: Revolutionary song, now French national anthem
- **Liberty Cap**: Phrygian cap symbolizing freedom
- **Guillotine**: Execution device symbolizing revolutionary justice
- **Motto**: "Liberté, égalité, fraternité" (Liberty, Equality, Fraternity)

## Controversies and Debates

Historians continue to debate:
- Was the Terror necessary or excessive?
- Did the Revolution achieve its goals?
- What was the role of economic vs. ideological factors?
- How much did it really change French society?
- Was it a bourgeois or popular revolution?

## Comparison with Other Revolutions

**American Revolution** (1776):
- Similarity: Enlightenment ideals, representative government
- Difference: Less radical social change, no reign of terror

**Russian Revolution** (1917):
- Similarity: Overthrow of monarchy, radical social change, period of terror
- Difference: Communist vs. liberal ideals

The French Revolution remains one of history's most studied events, representing both the promise of human liberty and the dangers of revolutionary extremism. Its legacy continues to shape political thought and democratic movements worldwide.
