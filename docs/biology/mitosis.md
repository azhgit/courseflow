# Mitosis: The Process of Cell Division

## What is Mitosis?

Mitosis is a type of cell division that results in two daughter cells, each having the same number and kind of chromosomes as the parent nucleus. This process is fundamental for growth, tissue repair, and asexual reproduction in eukaryotic organisms.

## The Cell Cycle

Mitosis is part of the larger cell cycle, which consists of:
- **Interphase**: The longest phase where the cell grows and DNA is replicated (G1, S, G2 phases)
- **Mitotic Phase (M phase)**: Where the actual division occurs
- **Cytokinesis**: The division of the cytoplasm

## The Stages of Mitosis

### Prophase
- Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes
- Each chromosome consists of two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere
- The nuclear envelope begins to break down
- Centrioles move to opposite poles of the cell
- Spindle fibers begin to form

### Metaphase
- Chromosomes align at the cell's equator, forming the metaphase plate
- Spindle fibers attach to the centromere of each chromosome at structures called kinetochores
- This is the checkpoint where the cell ensures all chromosomes are properly attached

### Anaphase
- Sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles of the cell
- The spindle fibers shorten, pulling chromatids apart
- The cell elongates
- This is typically the shortest phase of mitosis

### Telophase
- Chromosomes begin to decondense back into chromatin
- Nuclear envelopes reform around each set of chromosomes
- Spindle fibers disappear
- The cell prepares for cytokinesis

### Cytokinesis
- The cytoplasm divides, creating two separate cells
- In animal cells, a cleavage furrow forms
- In plant cells, a cell plate forms between the two nuclei
- Each daughter cell receives approximately half of the organelles

## Importance of Mitosis

1. **Growth**: Allows multicellular organisms to grow from a single cell
2. **Tissue Repair**: Replaces damaged or dead cells
3. **Asexual Reproduction**: Some organisms reproduce through mitosis
4. **Genetic Stability**: Ensures daughter cells are genetically identical to the parent

## Mitosis vs. Meiosis

While mitosis produces two identical diploid cells, meiosis produces four genetically different haploid cells (gametes). Mitosis is used for growth and repair, while meiosis is used for sexual reproduction.

## Regulation of Mitosis

The cell cycle is tightly regulated by:
- **Cyclins and CDKs**: Proteins that control progression through the cell cycle
- **Checkpoints**: Quality control mechanisms that ensure the cell is ready to proceed
- **Tumor suppressors**: Genes like p53 that can halt the cycle if problems are detected

Errors in mitosis regulation can lead to uncontrolled cell division and cancer.
